# Section F — Financial Plan and Estimated Budget

## F.1 What This Section Answers

- What does it actually cost to run REMA once?
- What does it cost to sustain REMA year after year?
- How is the budget allocated across phases and cost categories?
- Where are the biggest cost risks?
- What can be reduced, and what cannot?

---

## F.2 Budget Architecture

REMA's costs fall into three distinct buckets:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  BUCKET 1 — SETUP COSTS (one-time)                  │
│  Paid once to establish REMA. Not repeated.         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  BUCKET 2 — ANNUAL PREPAREDNESS COSTS               │
│  Paid every dry season to keep REMA ready.          │
│  Paid whether or not a flood occurs.                │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  BUCKET 3 — ACTIVATION COSTS (per flood event)      │
│  Paid only when REMA activates.                     │
│  Scales with flood severity and districts affected. │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Currency:** All costs in Vietnamese Dong (VND) with USD equivalent at **25,000 VND = 1 USD**.

Bucket 2 must be funded from the **operating budget** every year. Bucket 3 is funded from **emergency response reserves** or donor appeals.

### F.4.3 EMK Restocking (Post-Flood Replenishment)

This is the largest annual cost. After each flood, used EMKs must be replaced to return to pre-event stock levels.

**Planning assumption:** Each flood consumes approximately **70% of deployed stock** across 3 districts.

**EMK Unit Cost Breakdown:**

| EMK Type | Contents Cost (VND) | Packaging + Assembly (VND) | Total Unit Cost (VND) | Total Unit Cost (USD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMK-1 (General) | 75,000 | 15,000 | 90,000 | $3.60 |
| EMK-2 (Vulnerable) | 120,000 | 20,000 | 140,000 | $5.60 |
| EMK-3 (Chronic Illness) | 180,000 | 25,000 | 205,000 | $8.20 |

**Annual Restocking Cost (70% of full deployment):**

| EMK Type | Total Pre-Event Stock | 70% Replenishment | Unit Cost (VND) | Restocking Cost (VND) | Restocking Cost (USD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMK-1 | 15,910 units | 11,137 units | 90,000 | 1,002,330,000 | $40,093 |
| EMK-2 | 4,100 units | 2,870 units | 140,000 | 401,800,000 | $16,072 |
| EMK-3 | 1,200 units | 840 units | 205,000 | 172,200,000 | $6,888 |
| **Subtotal** | | | | **1,576,330,000** | **$63,053** |

> **Assumption F-3:** 70% consumption rate is the planning assumption for a full 3-district activation. In a severe event, restocking could reach 90% — add ~$18,000 as contingency buffer.

> **Assumption F-4:** EMK contents are procured at bulk rates through pre-signed agreements with pharmacy distributors. Bulk pricing is assumed to be 15–20% below retail. Without bulk agreements, unit costs increase by ~15% across all EMK types.

## F.8 Funding Sources and Strategy

| Funding Source | What It Covers | Likelihood | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viet Nam Red Cross provincial budget | Bucket 2 (partial) — training, admin, staff stipends | High | Core operating cost — should be baseline funded |
| Vietnam Red Cross Society national grants | Bucket 2 (EMK restocking) | Medium | Annual application required |
| International Red Cross / IFRC support | Bucket 1 (setup) + EMK restocking top-up | Medium | One-time setup grant is a strong ask |
| Ministry of Health co-funding | EMK-3 medication costs (part of national emergency health reserve) | Medium-High | MoH has existing emergency drug budget |
| Corporate / private donors (Vietnam) | Bucket 3 activation costs | Medium | Vietnamese corporate CSR programs active in disaster response |
| Local government (district level) | Sub-warehouse facility costs (in-kind) | High | Ward People's Committees provide space at no cost |

> **Assumption F-7:** The largest single line item — EMK restocking (~$63,000/year) — cannot be covered by Red Cross alone and requires either national Red Cross grants or MoH co-funding. This is the primary fundraising dependency and the highest budget risk.

## F.9 Budget Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Financial Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMK bulk pricing not secured — retail procurement required | Medium | +15% on EMK costs (~+$9,500/year) | Prioritize pre-signed distributor agreements in Phase 0 |
| Second flood activation in same year | Low-Medium | +$4,367 per additional activation | Hold 1 activation's worth of budget in reserve |
| Volunteer attrition forces paid recruitment | Low | +$2,000–5,000 for training new cohort | Maintain 20% roster buffer; invest in volunteer retention |
| Radio equipment damage during flood | Medium | +$500 for replacement | Waterproof equipment specified in Bucket 1; spare unit held |
| MoH medication reserve not released in time | Low | +$2,000–4,000 for commercial pharmacy emergency procurement | Pre-signed pharmacy backup agreements cover this |
| Currency fluctuation (imported medical supplies) | Low | ±10% on EMK-3 contents | Price agreements in VND where possible |

## F.10 What Cannot Be Cut

| Line Item | Why It Cannot Be Cut |
|---|---|
| EMK-3 medication restocking | Life-sustaining medication is not optional — cutting this directly harms chronically ill beneficiaries |
| Volunteer training (×2/year) | Undertrained volunteers make dangerous prioritization errors and safety mistakes |
| Radio equipment maintenance | Without backup communications, coordination fails when internet goes down |
| Hub Manager activation stipends | Unpaid Hub Managers are unreliable Hub Managers — this is a coordination risk, not just an HR issue |

### What Can Be Reduced If Budget Is Constrained

| Line Item | Reduction Option | Trade-Off |
|---|---|---|
| Volunteer training frequency | Reduce from 2× to 1× per year | Increases risk of errors, especially with new volunteers |
| EMK-1 stock levels | Reduce by 10–15%, increase reliance on community collection | Shifts burden to households — less equitable for immobile residents |
| Operational contingency reserve | Reduce from 15% to 10% | Less buffer for unexpected costs; higher financial risk |
| Volunteer meal provision | Replace with dry ration bags (cheaper) | Minor — acceptable trade-off with minimal operational impact |

## F.11 Key Decisions Summary

| Decision | Choice Made |
|---|---|
| Budget architecture | Three buckets: one-time setup / annual preparedness / per-activation |
| Currency | VND primary, USD equivalent at 25,000:1 |
| Year 1 total cost | ~$71,406 |
| Ongoing annual cost | ~$69,522 |
| 3-year total cost of ownership | ~$210,450 |
| Cost per beneficiary (full annual) | ~$0.95/person/year |
| Largest cost driver | EMK restocking (~91% of annual preparedness budget) |
| Primary funding risk | EMK restocking requires external co-funding (MoH or national Red Cross grants) |
| Non-negotiable lines | EMK-3 medication, volunteer training, radio maintenance, Hub Manager stipends |
| Cost reduction options | Training frequency, EMK-1 levels, contingency reserve size |
