# Section B - Logistics Model

## B.1. REMA uses **three EMK types**

### EMK-1 — General Household Kit

Target: Average household with no special medical needs
Pack size: 1 box, ~5kg, fits in a backpack

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) sachets | 20 sachets |
| Water purification tablets | 30 tablets |
| Basic wound care (bandages, antiseptic, gauze) | 1 set |
| Paracetamol (500mg) | 20 tablets |
| Antihistamine tablets | 10 tablets |
| Waterproof hygiene bag (soap, toothbrush, sanitary pads) | 1 set |
| Printed health instruction card (Vietnamese) | 1 card
 |

### EMK-2 — Vulnerable Household Kit

Target: Households with elderly, pregnant women, infants, or disabled persons
Pack size: 1 box, ~7kg

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| All items in EMK-1 | Full set |
| Infant oral rehydration formula | 5 sachets |
| Prenatal vitamins | 14 tablets |
| Adult incontinence pads | 5 pads |
| Thermometer | 1 |
| Blood pressure monitoring card + instructions | 1 set |
| Emergency contact card (nearest health facility, Red Cross hotline) | 1 card |

### EMK-3 — Chronic Illness Bridge Kit

Target: Households where a member has a known chronic illness (diabetes, hypertension, heart condition) and has lost access to their regular medication
Pack size: 1 small sealed bag, ~2kg

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| 3-day supply of common chronic illness medication (pre-agreed list with Ministry of Health) | 1 course |
| Blood glucose test strips | 10 strips |
| Sterile syringes (for insulin users) | 5 units |
| Referral card to nearest functioning health facility | 1 card |

## B.2. Sourcing — Where Supplies Come From

REMA uses a **three-source model** to reduce dependency on any single supplier:

| Source | What It Provides | When It's Used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Red Cross pre-stock** | EMK-1 and EMK-2 items stored year-round in central warehouse | Primary source — used first |
| **Ministry of Health emergency reserve** | EMK-3 medications and medical equipment | Activated within 6 hours of REMA Phase 1 trigger |
| **Pre-agreed commercial suppliers** (pharmacies, medical distributors) | Resupply of any item running low after Hour 24 | Backup — activated only if pre-stock is insufficient |

## B.3. Storage — How Stock Is Held Across Three Layers

### Layer 1 — Central Warehouse

- Permanent Red Cross facility, located above flood level
- Holds 100% of pre-stock before Phase 1 activates
- Organized into **pre-labeled dispatch pallets** — one pallet per sub-warehouse, pre-sorted by EMK type
- Maintains a 30% reserve after dispatching to sub-warehouses (for resupply in Phase 2)
- Managed by Warehouse Manager + 2 staff

### Layer 2 — Sub-Warehouses (×3)

- Temporary setup inside existing community buildings (ward office, school, health station)
- Each sub-warehouse requires approximately **40–60 square meters** of dry floor space
- Stock laid out on raised pallets or tables — never directly on the floor
- Managed by one Hub Manager + one inventory volunteer
- Printed stock ledger maintained at all times — updated every 4 hours

**Sub-warehouse setup checklist (completed during Phase 1, Hours 3–8):**

1. Receive EMK pallets from central warehouse truck
2. Verify count against printed dispatch manifest
3. Separate EMK-1, EMK-2, EMK-3 into clearly labeled zones
4. Set up volunteer check-in table at entrance
5. Post printed district map with delivery zones on wall
6. Confirm radio/phone contact with Operations Center

### Layer 3 — Volunteer Carry Capacity (Last Mile)

Volunteers do not store supplies — they carry and deliver. Each volunteer team carries:

| Delivery Mode | Carry Capacity | EMKs Per Trip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volunteer on foot (backpack) | ~10kg | 2× EMK-1 or 1× EMK-2 |
| Motorbike with pannier bags | ~25kg | 5× EMK-1 or 3× EMK-2 |
| Cargo bicycle | ~20kg | 4× EMK-1 or 2× EMK-2 |
| Small motorized boat | ~150kg | 30× EMK-1 or 20× EMK-2 |

## B.4. Transport — Moving Stock Through the Three Layers

### Layer 1 → Layer 2 (Central Warehouse to Sub-Warehouses)

**Mode:** Truck (Red Cross fleet + contracted vehicles)
**Timing:** Phase 1, Hours 3–8 — must complete before roads deteriorate
**Route principle:** Use the highest-elevation road routes, not shortest routes

| Route Decision Rule | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Avoid roads below 1.5m elevation | These flood first |
| Prefer concrete roads over asphalt | Asphalt degrades faster under flood pressure |
| Send convoy of 2+ trucks together | If one breaks down, the other completes delivery |
| Hub Manager confirms receipt within 30 minutes of arrival | Ensures no delivery is lost without record |

### Layer 2 → Layer 3 (Sub-Warehouse to Households)

**Mode:** Tiered by water depth (as defined in Section A)

Water 0–30cm   →   Motorbike

Water 30–60cm  →   Cargo bicycle or volunteer on foot

Water 60–80cm  →   Small motorized boat

Water >80cm    →   Delivery suspended, escalate to civil defense

**Delivery team structure per sub-warehouse:**

- 3 volunteer teams of 4 people each = 12 volunteers per sub-warehouse
- Each team assigned to one delivery zone (pre-mapped ward boundaries)
- Teams depart sub-warehouse at fixed times: 7am, 11am, 3pm
- Return to sub-warehouse to restock and report between runs

## B.5. Last-Mile Delivery Model

### How a Delivery Run Works

1. Team Leader collects household list from Hub Manager
    
    (prioritized list from volunteer assessment forms — see Section C)
    
2. Team loads EMKs onto their transport mode
3. Team follows pre-mapped route for their zone
4. At each household:
    - Confirm household identity (name + address on list)
    - Deliver correct EMK type (EMK-1, 2, or 3)
    - Household signs or thumbprints printed receipt
    - Volunteer notes any new critical cases not on list
5. Return to sub-warehouse:
    - Submit signed receipts to Hub Manager
    - Report new critical cases verbally
    - Restock for next run if needed

### Community Collection Points

For neighborhoods where water is below 30cm and households are mobile, REMA sets up **community collection points** — a fixed location (e.g., a dry street corner, a temple entrance) where residents come to collect their EMK rather than receiving door-to-door delivery.

## B.6. Contingency Plan — When Routes Fail

This is one of the six things judges explicitly look for. REMA has a **three-level contingency response:**

### Level 1 — Single Route Blocked

**Situation:** One delivery route within a zone is impassable.
**Response:**

- Volunteer team reroutes using the next available path on their pre-printed zone map (all zone maps include 2 alternate routes)
- No approval needed — team leader decides on the spot
- Blockage reported to Hub Manager at next check-in

### Level 2 — Entire Zone Inaccessible

**Situation:** An entire delivery zone cannot be reached by any surface route.
**Response:**

- Hub Manager requests boat deployment from the sub-warehouse boat allocation
- If no boat available at that sub-warehouse, Hub Manager contacts Operations Center to request boat transfer from another sub-warehouse
- If boat transfer takes more than 4 hours, affected zone households are contacted by phone (if network available) and told to move to nearest community collection point if safely possible
- Operations Center logs zone as "inaccessible" and escalates to local civil defense for evacuation support

### Level 3 — Sub-Warehouse Itself Inaccessible or Flooded

**Situation:** The sub-warehouse building floods or becomes unreachable.
**Response:**

- Hub Manager activates **backup sub-warehouse location** — a second site pre-identified during Phase 0 for exactly this scenario
- Central warehouse dispatches emergency resupply to backup location
- All volunteer teams in that district are notified of new pickup point via SMS or pre-agreed radio channel
- Operations Center updates dashboard to reflect new sub-warehouse location

## B.7 — Storage Conditions and Stock Integrity

### Why This Matters

EMKs are assembled months before a flood event and stored in community buildings with no guaranteed climate control. Vietnam's urban heat and humidity (average 28–35°C, 70–90% humidity in flood season) can degrade medications and supplies if storage conditions are not managed. This section defines minimum storage standards per EMK type and the stock rotation protocol.

---

### Storage Requirements by EMK Type

| EMK Type | Temperature Range | Humidity Limit | Special Requirement | Risk if Violated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EMK-1 (General) | 15–30°C | <80% | Keep dry, away from direct sunlight | ORS sachets clump; paracetamol degrades slowly |
| EMK-2 (Vulnerable) | 15–30°C | <80% | Same as EMK-1; thermometer must not be pre-exposed to heat | Thermometer accuracy affected above 40°C |
| EMK-3 (Chronic Illness) | **2–8°C (cold chain)** | <75% | **Not pre-stored at sub-warehouses** — see below | Insulin denatures above 25°C; test strips degrade in humidity |

---

### The EMK-3 Cold Chain Rule

EMK-3 is the only kit that requires cold chain. REMA's sub-warehouses are community buildings — they cannot reliably maintain 2–8°C.

**Solution: EMK-3 is never pre-positioned at sub-warehouses.**

Instead:

```bash
Phase 0 (Dry Season)
└── EMK-3 medications stored at Ministry of Health
    district cold storage facility year-round

Phase 1 (Hour 0–8, Flood Activation)
└── Operations Center requests MoH release of EMK-3 stock
└── MoH transfers directly to sub-warehouses in insulated
    cold boxes within 6 hours of activation

Phase 2 (Delivery)
└── EMK-3 delivered from sub-warehouse within 12 hours
    of MoH transfer — no overnight storage at sub-warehouse
```

---

### Expiry Management — FEFO Protocol

**FEFO = First Expired, First Out.** Items closest to their expiry date are used first.

| Stage | Action | Who | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assembly | Each EMK is labeled with the earliest expiry date of any item inside | Warehouse Manager | During dry-season assembly |
| Storage check | Items expiring within 3 months are flagged and moved to front of stock | Warehouse Manager | Monthly during flood season |
| Pre-event audit | Full expiry check of all EMK-1 and EMK-2 stock | Warehouse Manager | 4 weeks before flood season opens |
| Disposal | Expired items removed and replaced before flood season | Warehouse Manager | As flagged |
| EMK-3 | Expiry managed by MoH — Red Cross verifies expiry on receipt at activation | Hub Manager | At time of MoH transfer |

### Sub-Warehouse Physical Storage Standards

Even without refrigeration, sub-warehouse conditions can be controlled to protect EMK-1 and EMK-2:

| Standard | Requirement | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raised storage | All EMKs stored minimum 30cm off the floor on pallets or tables | Protects against water ingress and ground moisture |
| Away from walls | Minimum 10cm gap between EMK stacks and walls | Prevents condensation damage |
| No direct sunlight | Covered with tarpaulin if windows expose stock to direct sun | Heat and UV degrade packaging and contents |
| Ventilation | Room must have at least one open vent or window | Reduces humidity buildup |
| Waterproof outer wrap | Each EMK pallet wrapped in plastic sheeting | Last line of defense if sub-warehouse takes minor water ingress |

---

### Stock Integrity Check on Receipt

When a Hub Manager receives EMKs at the sub-warehouse, they must verify:

1. Box seals are intact — no damaged or opened kits
2. No visible water damage to outer packaging
3. Spot-check 5% of boxes — open and verify contents match manifest
4. For EMK-3 received from MoH — verify cold box seal intact and temperature log shows no excursion above 8°C

Any failed check → reject the batch and notify Operations Center immediately.
