# Section C - Prioritization Framework

## C.1 What This Section Answers

- Which supplies are prioritized when stock is limited?
- Which population groups are prioritized, and why?
- How is urgency balanced against fairness?
- How does a volunteer actually score a household in the field?
- What happens when two households have equal scores?

---

## C.2 Why Prioritization Is Hard in This Context

Three things make prioritization genuinely difficult in a flood emergency:

**Incomplete information.** Volunteers don't know who needs what until they knock on doors. The vulnerability map from Phase 0 is a starting point, not a complete picture.

**Scarce resources.** REMA cannot serve everyone simultaneously. Choices about who gets EMK-2 or EMK-3 first are real trade-offs with real consequences.

**Fairness vs. urgency tension.** Pure medical urgency favors the sickest. Pure fairness favors equal distribution. REMA must balance both — and be able to explain every decision to the community and to auditors.

---

## C.3 Two Levels of Prioritization

```
LEVEL 1 — SUPPLY PRIORITY     Which EMK type goes first when stock is limited?
LEVEL 2 — HOUSEHOLD PRIORITY  Which household within a zone gets served first?
```

---

## C.4 Level 1 — Supply Priority

| Priority | EMK Type | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | EMK-3 (Chronic Illness Bridge Kit) | Running out of life-sustaining medication is immediately life-threatening |
| 2nd | EMK-2 (Vulnerable Household Kit) | Pregnant women, infants, elderly, disabled — highest risk of rapid deterioration |
| 3rd | EMK-1 (General Household Kit) | Important but not immediately life-threatening for otherwise healthy adults |

> **Assumption C-1:** Resupply of EMK-3 from the central warehouse reserve reaches any sub-warehouse within 4 hours. If roads are completely cut off, the Emergency Coordinator escalates to the Ministry of Health emergency reserve for direct delivery by boat.
>

## C.5 Level 2 — Household Priority Scoring System

Every household receives a **Vulnerability Score** from 0 to 20 during the assessment phase (Hours 8–16).

### Scoring Criteria

| # | Category | What the Volunteer Checks | Max Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Medical urgency** | Chronic illness + lost access to life-sustaining medication | 8 pts |
| 2 | **Household vulnerability** | Infants, pregnant women, elderly (65+), disabled persons | 5 pts |
| 3 | **Flood exposure** | Water depth at/in household; structural safety | 4 pts |
| 4 | **Self-sufficiency** | Access to food, clean water, sanitation | 2 pts |
| 5 | **Isolation** | Cut off from neighbors, family, communication | 1 pt |
|  | **TOTAL** |  | **20 pts** |

### Category 1 — Medical Urgency (max 8 points)

| Situation | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Life-sustaining medication (insulin, heart medication) — run out or <24h remaining | 8 |
| Chronic illness — medication low (1–2 days remaining) | 5 |
| Chronic illness — medication currently adequate | 2 |
| No chronic illness reported | 0 |

### Category 2 — Household Vulnerability (max 5 points)

| Situation | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Infant under 2 years present | +2 |
| Pregnant woman present | +2 |
| Elderly person (65+) living alone | +2 |
| Person with physical or cognitive disability present | +2 |
| *Cap at 5 points regardless of combinations* | Max 5 |

### Category 3 — Flood Exposure (max 4 points)

| Situation | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Water inside household or building structurally unsafe | 4 |
| Water at doorstep (within 10cm of entrance) | 3 |
| Water in street but not reaching household | 1 |
| Household is dry and elevated | 0 |

### Category 4 — Self-Sufficiency (max 2 points)

| Situation | Points |
| --- | --- |
| No clean water, no food, no sanitation access | 2 |
| Partial access | 1 |
| Adequate access to basic necessities | 0 |

### Category 5 — Isolation (max 1 point)

| Situation | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Completely isolated — no neighbors, no family, no phone signal | 1 |
| Some social contact or communication available | 0 |

### Score Bands and Delivery Priority

| Score | Band | Delivery Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15–20 | 🔴 Critical | Deliver within the current run — goes first |
| 10–14 | 🟠 High | Deliver in the same day |
| 5–9 | 🟡 Medium | Deliver within 48 hours |
| 0–4 | 🟢 Standard | Community collection point self-pickup |

---

## C.6 Tiebreaker Rules

When two households have the same score, apply in order:

1. Higher Category 1 score wins — medical urgency always takes precedence
2. Household with infant under 6 months wins
3. Household whose assessment form was submitted first wins

Rules are printed on the back of every volunteer's assessment form. No discretion — fully mechanical.

---

## C.7 Fairness Safeguards

| Safeguard | How It Works |
| --- | --- |
| Written scores | Every score is recorded on a physical form — Hub Managers can audit any delivery decision |
| Cross-ward assignment | Volunteers assess wards they are not from — reduces favoritism |
| Collection points always open | Any household can collect EMK-1 regardless of score |
| Score challenge mechanism | Households can report to Hub Manager; score reviewed and adjusted if error found |
| No permanent exclusion | Score 0–4 = standard priority, not no delivery |

> **Assumption C-2:** 36 volunteers total (12 per sub-warehouse × 3 districts) allows cross-ward assignment without leaving any ward uncovered.
>

## C.8 Worked Example

| Household | Cat 1 | Cat 2 | Cat 3 | Cat 4 | Cat 5 | Total | Band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — elderly man, 72, alone, no medication issues | 0 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 8 | 🟡 Medium |
| B — family of 4, infant 8 months, water at doorstep | 0 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 0 | 6 | 🟡 Medium |
| C — woman, 45, diabetic, ran out of insulin yesterday | 8 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 9 | 🟡 Medium |
| D — pregnant woman, water inside household | 0 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 0 | 8 | 🟡 Medium |
| E — family of 5, no chronic illness, dry street | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 🟢 Standard |
| F — elderly woman, 68, hypertension, medication low | 5 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 10 | 🟠 High |

**Delivery order:** F (EMK-3+EMK-2) → C (EMK-3) → D (EMK-2) → A (EMK-1) → B (EMK-1) → E (collection point)

## C.9 Scarcity Mode

Triggered when total stock across all sub-warehouses falls below 30% of original allocation:

- EMK-1 community collection points temporarily suspended for Standard band
- All remaining EMK-1 redirected to Medium band and above
- Emergency Coordinator notifies Ministry of Health and requests emergency resupply
- Communities informed via ward loudspeaker system

> **Assumption C-3:** Each ward has a community loudspeaker system for public announcements — a standard feature of Vietnamese ward administration.
>

## C.10 Key Decisions Summary

| Decision | Choice Made |
| --- | --- |
| Prioritization levels | Two levels: supply type priority + household delivery priority |
| Scoring system | 5-category, 20-point scale on paper assessment form |
| Highest-weight criterion | Medical urgency (Category 1, max 8 points) |
| Score bands | Critical / High / Medium / Standard |
| Tiebreakers | Cat 1 score → infant under 6 months → first form submitted |
| Community collection points | Always open regardless of score |
| Fairness safeguards | Cross-ward assignment, written scores, challenge mechanism, no permanent exclusion |
| Scarcity mode trigger | Total stock below 30% of original allocation |
